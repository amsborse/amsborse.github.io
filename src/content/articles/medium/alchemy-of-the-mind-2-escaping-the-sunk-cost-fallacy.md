The other day, I was watching a TV show. I wasn’t really enjoying it — I liked the first episode, but from there, it went downhill. Yet, I kept watching. Why? Because I had already invested three hours into it. If I stopped now, those three hours would feel wasted. Why was I willing to waste **seven more hours** just to justify the first three? That didn’t make sense. But awareness helped me notice it.

<blockquote class="medium-pullquote"><p>The cost of staying stuck is always greater than the cost of walking away.</p></blockquote>

Curious, I started researching and — voilà! — I discovered the sunk cost fallacy: the cognitive bias where past investments — time, money, effort — keep us tethered to irrational decision-making. We stay in dead-end jobs because we’ve already put in years. We hold onto toxic relationships because we “can’t just throw it all away.” We keep watching a terrible movie because, well, we’re already an hour in. We keep reading a boring book because we have already started it

## The Paradox of Sunk Costs

We think we’re being rational, but really, we’re negotiating with our own past choices. Instead of evaluating what serves us **now**, we let **what we’ve already lost** dictate what we continue to lose. The paradox?

<blockquote class="medium-pullquote"><p>The more we cling, the deeper we sink.</p></blockquote>

Imagine a gambler trying to win back lost money. Every additional bet isn’t a fresh decision — it’s an emotional ransom for the money already gone. The same happens with our time and energy. We’d rather chase sunk costs than cut our losses and move forward.

## The Psychology Behind the Bias

Your brain hates waste. The sunk cost fallacy is a feature, not a bug — it evolved to help us persist in the face of adversity. The problem? Not all persistence is wise. There’s a difference between **grit** and **stubborn self-sabotage**. The key is knowing when to pivot.

## Overcoming the Sunk Cost Trap

1. **Ask the Brutal Question:** If you were making this decision **from scratch**, would you still choose it? If the answer is no, your past is holding your future hostage.
2. **Separate Emotion from Logic:** Recognize the pull of sunk costs. Feel it, but don’t let it steer the wheel.
3. **Redefine “Quitting” as Winning:** Walking away isn’t failure — it’s strategy. Smart investors cut bad stocks. Smart people cut bad decisions.
4. **Flip the Perspective:** Instead of focusing on what you’ve already spent, focus on what you’ll keep losing if you don’t let go.

## Flip the Perspective

You owe nothing to your past — what’s spent is spent. Time, money, energy — it’s all gone. But your next move? That’s still in your hands.

Life isn’t an algorithm with guaranteed outcomes; it’s an experiment. And experiments fail — that’s the point. If every action yielded predictable results, it wouldn’t be called growth; it’d be a script.

<blockquote class="medium-pullquote"><p>But life isn’t scripted — it’s trial and error, chaos and discovery.</p></blockquote>

If there were a formula for everything, where’s the adventure in that?

Life is short. Let go. Move forward. The cost of staying stuck is always greater than the cost of walking away.
