## ❓ The Big Question

Everyone talks about purpose and goals. Find it. Chase it. Live it.

But here’s the question most never pause to ask:<br>**Should you even have a goal or a purpose at all?**

What if having a goal makes you a slave to a future version of yourself — one you no longer recognize?<br>What if the real reason you feel stuck, stressed, or behind…<br>…is because you’re forcing yourself toward a finish line you no longer care about?

Let’s take an honest look.

## 😢 The Prison of Past Goals

You chose your “goal” at 10, 15, 21. You inherited it from your parents, your culture, or a desperate moment. And now? You’re dragging it around like a broken compass, wondering why you feel lost.

The truth is, **many people are slaves to goals they no longer believe in** — but they’re too afraid to admit it. Because starting over feels like failure.<br>So they keep going. Climbing a ladder that might be leaning against the wrong wall.<br>Now you’re carrying it like debt — out of guilt, not joy.

You’ve outgrown the reasons behind it, but never paused to renegotiate.<br>Why? Because most people confuse _consistency_ with _loyalty to a dead idea_.

> _“Most people aren’t stuck in life. They’re stuck in an outdated story of who they think they should be.”_

## 👁️‍🗨️ When Life Starts to Feel Like a Drag

Here’s what happens when you keep chasing a goal that no longer fits:<br>Stress. Anxiety. Resistance. Burnout. Existential dread masked as productivity.

You think you’re just tired. But what if you’re resisting the very thing you _chose_?

You keep asking, <em>“Why is life so hard?”</em><br>But the real question is: _“Am I growing fast enough to handle what life is asking of me?”_

Stress. Burnout. The Sunday Scaries.<br>They’re not always about the job.<br>Sometimes, they’re about the fact that you’re moving forward… with friction.

You made a choice long ago, and now you’re too afraid to revise it.<br>But here’s the problem: **the world changes every day** — and if you don’t change with it, life becomes hard.

> You’re not overwhelmed because life is too much.<em><br></em>You’re overwhelmed because **you stayed the same while life moved on**.

## 💪 Upgrade Yourself, Then Choose Again

Before you abandon your goal — ask:

_Is the goal wrong_

- _Or am I just too tired to enjoy it?_
- _Or the energy chasing it?_
- _Or do I just hate how I feel chasing it?”_

If life feels heavy, don’t throw the goal away — first, **upgrade the system chasing it**.

A race car doesn’t blame the track when its engine is weak.

First, upgrade your system:

- Your body = Your engine (Sleep, food, movement).
- Your mind = Your steering wheel (Focus, learning, mindset).
- Your awareness = The driver (The _you_ — the one choosing, adjusting, reflecting — _you_ are the driver)

And guess what? A tired engine can’t enjoy the drive. A foggy windshield can’t see the road.

So before you chase clarity, chase **capacity**.<br>Build energy. Refine attention. Learn to be _here_. Then see if the goal still calls to you.

> <em>When your body is strong and your mind is sharp, goals become games.<br>When you’re tired and anxious, even games feel like survival.</em>

A tired body can’t carry a beautiful vision.<br>A mind running on old patterns will turn even a good goal into suffering.

So focus first on becoming whole — physically sharp, mentally alive.<br>Then choose again. From a place of strength, not survival.

## 🧭 “How to Set a Goal That’s Actually Worth It”

Most people set goals the same way they shop when hungry — everything looks good, nothing sustains.

That’s because your **clarity in the present** determines your **wisdom about the future**.

So before you write down a goal, ask yourself:

- What problem in me is this goal trying to solve?
- Is this a goal I _want_ — or one I _feel behind on_?
- If I reached it tomorrow, would it feel like _relief_ or _joy_?

A real goal isn’t an escape. It’s an extension. It should feel like the natural next chapter of who you are today — not a fantasy meant to distract you from who you don’t want to be.

The more clarity you have, the more your goals will:

- Align instead of pull
- Expand instead of restrict
- Energize instead of exhaust

> <em>Don’t set a goal to fix yourself.<br> Set a goal to</em> **_express the most alive version of you._**

## ✨ How to Set Goals That Evolve With You

Don’t chase goals to feel complete.<br>**Feel complete — and then set goals as an expression of that wholeness.**

Instead of rigid timelines and narrow lanes, pick a **direction**, not a destination.

Like:

- “I want to help others.”
- “I want to explore ideas.”
- “I want to build useful things.”

Each of those gives you _infinite creative paths_: medicine, writing, teaching, startups, mentoring.<br>When your direction is broad, your path can be flexible.<br>**Creativity is just your curiosity having options.**

When your goal is a direction, not a fixed point, you’re free to adapt.

## 🧘 Be Here. Adjust Often.

The world changes. People change. You change.

So your beliefs, goals, and opinions? They should evolve too.

Don’t get stuck in yesterday’s answers. Learn to check in. Stay present.<br>Because **the real superpower is real-time awareness.** That’s how you catch misalignment before it becomes misery.

> _The most dangerous kind of stuck is the one that_ looks _like success but_ feels _like drag._

Take time — every day, or at least every week — to sit with this question:<br>“Does this still feel right?”

Update your direction like you update your apps.<br>Your life deserves real-time alignment.

> _The best way to chase the future… is to be fully present now._

Update like an app. Small tweaks \> complete overhauls.

A goal should pull you forward, not chain you down.

## ✅ So, Should You Have a Goal?

Yes.<br>And no.

If you feel incomplete — chasing a goal will feel like walking with chains and weights.<br>**Don’t start with the goal. Start with yourself.**

Build your body. Sharpen your mind. Feel alive again.

<strong>Then, from that place of fullness, set a direction — not a destination.</strong><br>Make it broad enough to explore, strong enough to adapt, and light enough to carry with joy.

Only then will your goals **carry you forward**, instead of weighing you down.

When you’re aligned, the right goals won’t feel like burdens.<br>They’ll feel like play.

So — should you have a goal?

Yes. But only if it’s _yours._ Only if it grows _with_ you.

And if it doesn’t?

It’s okay to change your mind.

Because the only real failure is staying stuck in a story that’s no longer yours.
