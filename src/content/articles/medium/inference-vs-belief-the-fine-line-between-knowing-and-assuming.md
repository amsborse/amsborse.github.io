Let’s kick this off with an admission that might raise an eyebrow: **“I don’t believe the Earth is round.”** Wait — what? Before you rush to judgment, let me clarify. I’m not saying I think the Earth is flat based on some unexamined belief. It’s just that, in my experience, the Earth has always felt… well, flat. It looks flat, it feels flat, and unless I’m on a plane at 30,000 feet, the curvature isn’t exactly noticeable to me.

But **“I _infer_ that the Earth is round.”** Why? Because science has gathered a mountain of evidence — photos from space, the way ships disappear over the horizon, the way planes chart courses around the globe. The list goes on, and each piece of data strengthens the inference that the Earth is, indeed, spherical.

Before you can break free from limiting beliefs, you need to recognize them in action. The more you catch yourself in these moments, the more you sharpen your ability to separate what you assume from what is actually real. And that shift? That’s where real growth begins.

### 🧠I Don’t Know vs I Know

Belief says, **“I don’t know, but I’m sure it’s true.”** It’s not asking for proof, not searching for validation. Your beliefs shape your world like a pair of tinted glasses. They filter everything you see and feel, whether it’s logical or not. Remember when people believed the Earth was flat? Sure, it felt true to them — until someone decided to challenge it.

Inference, on the other hand, says, **“I don’t know, but based on what I’ve seen, this might be true.”** It’s the act of drawing conclusions based on evidence, facts, or observations. It’s not about accepting things without questioning, but rather about connecting the dots logically. The key difference? **Inference is okay with being wrong.** It’s like saying, **“Here’s my best answer for now, but if new information comes along, I’m open to changing my mind.”**

### 🧠[Placebo ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placebo)vs. [Nocebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocebo): The Power of the Mind to Heal or Harm

It’s one of the strangest paradoxes of the human experience: The very belief in something’s power can shape the outcome — whether that thing is real or not. Take the placebo effect, for example. You’ve heard of it — when a person believes they are receiving a real treatment, but in reality, it’s just a sugar pill or a harmless saline injection. And yet, they feel better. Their symptoms improve. Their body responds as though the treatment were legit. Why? Because the mind believes it will work.

But the mind can also work against you. Enter the _nocebo effect_. It’s the opposite of the placebo, where negative beliefs or expectations about a treatment lead to real, physical symptoms of harm, even when there’s no cause. Ever heard someone say, “I think I’m getting sick,” and then, bam — they get sick? That’s nocebo in action. The mind has the power to make us sick with nothing more than a thought.

Here’s the thing about both: They show just how much influence our beliefs have over our physical health. It’s not just about wishing away the flu or thinking happy thoughts — this is about real, measurable change. And yet, in our everyday lives, we often ignore or underestimate the power of our own thoughts and beliefs. The mind is a powerful tool — either a healer or a saboteur. The question is: Which one will you choose to let it be?

### 🧠 Borrowed Beliefs: Digging your own grave

We’ve all been there, right? We’ve borrowed answers from books, gurus, and “successful” people who seem to have it all figured out. And sure, it feels good in the moment. The idea of someone else’s path leading to success can be a seductive comfort. But here’s the thing: borrowed answers rarely work in the long run. They’re like putting on a mask — it might look good at first, but it doesn’t reflect who you truly are.

Now, don’t get me wrong — learning from others can be a beautiful thing. But when we start adopting others’ truths without questioning them or making them our own, we risk losing touch with our authentic selves. Psychology tells us it’s easier to believe in things handed to us. It’s simpler to follow the latest trend, follow the loudest voice, or blindly emulate someone else’s success because, frankly, it requires less effort. It’s easier to conform than to carve your own path.

[No one can save you.](https://medium.com/@amsborse/no-one-is-coming-to-save-you-f133f9ffbf87) No one else can dictate your purpose, your truth, or your happiness. When you embrace that, you unlock a freedom that no borrowed belief could ever give you.

### 🧠 Believing is passive. Thinking? That takes energy.

Belief is passive. You don’t have to think too hard, question too much, or risk being wrong. It’s effortless. And, unfortunately, it’s often the easiest way to stay stuck.

Thinking? It demands your energy, your time, your openness. It challenges your assumptions and asks uncomfortable questions. It’s like lifting weights for your mind. It’s hard, but oh, is it worth it. Because when you think, when you push your boundaries, you don’t just grow — you transform.

Let’s explore some common beliefs that, in my experience, turned out to be false — and how challenging them transformed my perspective on the world.

### 🔒The 9-to-5 Myth: Who Tricked Us Into Thinking This Is Normal?

I remember talking to people around me about stress and anxiety, and the response was always the same — _“That’s just life.”_ But I couldn’t accept it. Since when did grinding 360 days a year just to escape for 5 days on a vacation become _normal_? **Who decided that exhaustion, anxiety, and perpetual busyness were just part of the deal?** Somewhere along the way, we bought into a carefully crafted illusion. Society conditioned us to believe that life is about endless work, that productivity is our sole purpose, and that a lifetime of labor is the price of existence.

### 🗝️From Worker to Creator

Now, let’s be clear — I’m not telling you to flip your desk, storm out of your job, and declare yourself free without a plan. Recklessness and freedom are not the same thing. Every action has consequences, and understanding those consequences is what sets you up for _real_ change. Because when you start making decisions from _understanding_ — not just belief — that’s when you stop being just a worker. We are _creators._ Deep down, every one of us has a spark — to build, to innovate, to express. Think about it. Remember when you made something — anything — just because you wanted to? No boss, no deadlines, just **pure creation? That’s what you were meant for.**

### 🔒You Are Not Your Labels: The Freedom of Being a Blank Slate

Here’s a hard pill to swallow: _You are not the labels you’ve given yourself._ But we love to box ourselves in, don’t we? “I’m a coffee person.” “I’m an introvert.” “I’m bad at math.” These aren’t identities; they’re just stories we keep repeating until we mistake them for truth. Think about it — you weren’t _born_ with these labels. You didn’t enter the world clutching a coffee cup, avoiding small talk, or struggling with algebra. You _became_ these things through experiences, habits, and beliefs that _felt_ true at some point. If your experiences had been different, so would _you_.

### 🗝️Law of attraction — But Are You Ready to believe?

**You attract what you believe, not what you want.** Here’s the hard truth: the universe is like a mirror. It doesn’t hand out answers or present opportunities just because you’re sitting back, waiting for them. It’s more like a cosmic GPS — it only kicks into gear when you start moving in the right direction. Because if you’re not asking, the universe can’t give. Like-minded people will show up in your life, just like they always have. Your circle mirrors your energy and interests. The same way you’re drawn to people who “get” you, others with similar passions and ambitions will gravitate toward you. You don’t have to look far — your circle is already forming, one meaningful connection at a time.

### 🔒**The Paradox of Belief: If You Knew, You Wouldn’t Believe**

Here’s the mind-bender: Belief means you don’t actually know. If you knew, it would be a fact — plain, simple, irrefutable. Belief is the fog that surrounds truth, the blurry space between what you think is real and what you can prove to be real. Think about it: we don’t believe in the ground beneath us — we know it’s there. Belief exists when we can’t grasp the full picture. **When you embrace the fact that belief is the gap between what you know and what you don’t, you free yourself from the pressure to be right or certain.**

### 🗝️**Know Yourself: The Power of Self-Discovery**

Think about the phrases you’ve heard a thousand times — “I believe in myself” or “I believe in my potential.” But hold on a second. If you truly knew your potential, if you could see it as clearly as you see the screen in front of you right now, would you _need_ to believe in yourself? No. You’d _know_ yourself. There would be no belief required. When you _know_ your limits, your strengths, and your potential firsthand, belief becomes irrelevant. You don’t need faith in what you can do — you _know_ what you’re capable of.

### 🔒Avoid Negativity? Sure, Just Ignore the Wisdom It’s Trying to Share!

Negative emotions? They’re not the enemy. In fact, they’re some of your most powerful allies. Think about it — when was the last time you learned something truly valuable when everything was going perfectly? Yeah, not often. We live in a world that tells us to “avoid negativity,” “think positive,” and “always stay happy.” But here’s the catch: negative emotions aren’t bad; they’re messengers, waiting to teach you something important. **Negative emotions are like unpaid bills — ignore them long enough, and they’ll come back to haunt you with compound interest.**

### 🗝️”Negative” — They’re Your Secret Weapon

Think of them as the red flags on the map to personal growth. Ignore them, and you might miss the turning point. Listen to them, and you’ll navigate the path with more clarity, wisdom, and understanding. The next time you’re hit with a wave of anger or disappointment, instead of labeling it “bad,” and distracting yourself, pause for a moment and ask, “What’s this really about?” You’ll be surprised at how much deeper you’ll get to know yourself. Negative emotions, in this light, become not obstacles but stepping stones.

### 🔒**You are a product of your past, and it defines who you are.**

We all love a good origin story, don’t we? “I’m like this because of my past.” “I act this way because of what happened before.” It’s easy to let our past dictate our present, like a pre-written script we’re helpless to rewrite. But here’s the plot twist: your past doesn’t hold the pen. You do.

### 🗝️ [Adlerian psychology](https://sunilsuri.com/couragetobedisliked) — Leave the past to the dead

Yes, your history influences you — no doubt about that. But it doesn’t have to lock you into a never-ending cycle of “this is just who I am.” The power to change, to grow, to rewrite the script? That’s in your hands. Your choices today are like the paintbrush to your canvas, creating the masterpiece of who you will become.

### 🔒**The Logic Trap: When Yesterday’s Truths Block Tomorrow’s Possibilities**

Your beliefs are shaped by what you’ve seen, what you’ve read, and what you’ve been told. Your logic? It’s shaped by a tiny fraction of your experiences from what’s actually out there. And yet, we go around acting like we’ve cracked the code of the universe.

We’re on the brink of setting up shop on Mars. Picture that: Earth’s logic might not even make sense on a different planet. The rules of gravity? Different. Farming methods? Outdated. Communication? Who knows. So, if we can throw out the logic of Earth and reinvent the rules on Mars, why are we so hell-bent on holding onto logic that’s long been obsolete?

### 🗝️<strong>“The Universe is Evolving— So Should Your Logic”</strong>

**Logic, like everything else, is fluid.** The facts you hold dear today could be outdated tomorrow — and that’s not a tragedy. It’s evolution. The moment you lock yourself into rigid ways of thinking, you block the very growth that could unlock deeper understanding. Life is never static. The universe is constantly evolving, so why wouldn’t your logic evolve with it?

### 🔒The Expectation Effect: How It Shapes Our Reality

What if I told you that it’s not your beliefs that determine your reality, but rather the expectations that those beliefs create? It’s like this sneaky little force that molds your life — whether you’re aware of it or not. Where your mind’s eye can actually shape the world around you by focusing on what you expect to happen. If you expect failure because you’ve convinced yourself you’re not cut out for a challenge, guess what? Your actions will align with that expectation, creating a loop where failure becomes your “truth.” On the flip side, inferring that you can improve by making adjustments and learning from mistakes builds a pathway to success, even if that success doesn’t come right away.

### 🗝️The Trap of Expectation: Why Acceptance is the Key

Expecting something, anything, is the very thing that keeps you from fully experiencing the beauty of what is? When you expect, you’re not simply hoping for an outcome; you’re _forcing_ the universe to deliver on your terms. You’re forcing a reality rather than accepting the one that’s unfolding before you. Your predictions are limited by your own perspective — your experiences, your knowledge, your narrow scope of what you’ve already seen. You’re only capable of expecting a handful of outcomes. And how often do those expectations match the reality? Rarely. And when they don’t, you’re left with disappointment, frustration, and negativity.

By accepting what is, you open yourself to seeing the full picture, not just the version you were attached to. The beauty is that every situation, no matter how it seems at first, has two sides — like the ☯︎[Yin and Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yin_and_yang), light and dark. And in each moment, you have a choice: to focus on the bright side, or get lost in the shadow of unfulfilled expectations.

When you expect, you’re bound for failure, because nothing ever aligns perfectly with your narrow view. But when you accept, you’re allowing life to show you its full spectrum — the highs and the lows, the unexpected gifts hidden in the struggles, and the lessons tucked away in moments of pain. Suddenly, you’re not disappointed because things didn’t go as planned; you’re grateful for whatever unfolds. You’re present, you’re peaceful, and you see the opportunities that come with every twist and turn.

### 🎁 Closing Thoughts

**You don’t have to choose one over the other.** While beliefs help us make sense of the world when we don’t have all the facts, inferences allow us to adapt and grow as we gather new information. Beliefs give us a sense of direction, helping us make sense of the world when we’re in the dark. They provide comfort when answers are scarce. But inferences? Inferences let us adapt, evolve, and keep moving forward. They’re not set in stone; they’re the stepping stones that guide us as we gather more information. In fact, the real power comes from finding harmony between the two.

Imagine this: you’re walking through life, beliefs in one hand and inferences in the other. Beliefs are your compass — they set your course, help you make decisions, and help you navigate uncertainty. But your inferences? They’re the map that adjusts as you go. Every new piece of information, every change in your environment, shifts your understanding and adjusts your direction. By testing your beliefs against reality — what’s really working in your life — we can move through life with greater clarity and purpose.

Don’t expect the answers to come overnight, though. Rome wasn’t built in a day, and neither will your clarity. But every day, no matter how distracted by work or life’s endless to-do lists, carve out time to think. Ten minutes is all it takes. A few moments to watch a video, read an article, or ask yourself a meaningful question — _that_ is the stuff that unlocks your growth.

As you begin to challenge outdated beliefs and embrace new perspectives, you’ll find that life becomes more exciting, more vibrant, and more full of possibility. It’s not always easy, but it is always worth it. Remember, success isn’t about fitting into someone else’s mold. A huge part of success, for me, was having the courage to question, adapt, and believe that I could carve my own path.
