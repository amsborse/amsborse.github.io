When I was a kid, my parents told me that when life feels hard, it means God is testing you.

At the time, I thought — “Really? Another test? As if the school exams weren’t enough.” It felt like there was this cosmic teacher grading me without telling me what syllabus I was supposed to study.

But now, I see things differently.

It wasn’t God handing out challenges. It was me — my own dreams, ambitions, and the silent contract I signed with life the moment I wished for something more.

The higher I aim, the more capable I need to become. And growth doesn’t come dressed as a reward — it often arrives wearing the face of discomfort.

Difficult situations? They’re not difficult in themselves. They become difficult when I resist doing what needs to be done. When I want to escape instead of engage.

* * *

I realized something fundamental: a situation feels negative **only** when I wish I were somewhere else. But that very desire — that longing — is actually a compass. It’s not punishment. It’s guidance.

If I want to be somewhere else, I need to move. Blaming the situation or others only delays the journey. The moment I accept the situation as a _mirror_, not a monster, something changes.

This isn’t divine test, its simply, _life responding to your intent_.

> The moment you stop running from the discomfort — and turn toward it — <br>the gap becomes a path.

* * *

Once I started taking consistent action toward the life I actually wanted, I stopped resenting the one I was in.

I stopped thinking of life as unfair. Because honestly, it isn’t.

Life’s just waiting — to see if you’re serious.

> Life doesn’t move because you wish it to. It moves when you begin to move with it.

You say you want something. Life says: “Okay.

Then become someone who can carry it.”

And if you somehow get what you didn’t work for, you’ll live in fear of losing it — because deep down, you’ll know you didn’t grow into it.

What arrives without effort rarely rests in peace. You will clutch it, not carry it. And life will feel like a thief, not a teacher.

* * *

So here’s the choice:<br>You can either lower your dreams to avoid difficulty. Or you can open your arms wide, take full responsibility, and grow into the person who can carry those dreams with confidence.

You can shrink the sky to fit your comfort, or you can grow until the sky no longer feels large.

We often say: “With great power comes great responsibility.” But it’s the opposite. **With great responsibility comes great power.**

Responsibility is what unlocks your next level. Power doesn’t arrive first. Ownership does. Once that’s in place — life, or God, or the universe, or the energy (whatever you believe in) — will meet you halfway.

Responsibility isn’t a burden. It’s the bridge. Step on it, and the ground beneath you will rise to meet your feet.

> Power is not handed down.<br>It rises.<br>From the quiet place where you finally stop blaming and start building.
