### ➳ The “I Know” Trap: How Confirmation Bias Stunts Your Growth

Sitting on my bed, I was posting a reel for my previous article. As it uploaded, I started scrolling — just killing time. That’s when I stumbled on an article. The title caught my eye, something about “rethinking what you know.”

I clicked.<br>I skimmed.<br>I disagreed.

_“This can’t be right.”_

Without even finishing, I scrolled to the comments, ready to see if others had pointed out the flaws I had _already decided_ were there. And then it hit me — **I wasn’t reading to learn. I was reading to confirm what I already believed.**

### ➵ The Ego’s Favorite Lie: “I Know”

Ever notice how saying _“I know”_ shuts down a conversation? The moment you declare knowledge, your brain **lowers the value of incoming information**, making it seem less important. Instead of absorbing, you start scanning for flaws, ready to counter with what you already believe.

Most minds engage with information in **egoic resistance**:

🚫 **Perceive → Judge → Defend**

But true wisdom unfolds through **conscious receptivity**:

✅ **Observe → Discern → Inquire → Internalize → Evolve**

Questioning isn’t disrespect. **True disrespect is judging before you understand.** Asking _why_ doesn’t make you difficult — it makes you **free**. A mind that doesn’t question is a mind enslaved.

### ➵ The Ultimate Hobby: Learning

You don’t know everything — and that’s a gift. The moment you think you do, you stop growing. Learning isn’t just about facts; it’s about seeing the beauty in understanding. The world is full of things you take for granted. When you start paying attention, you start becoming more yourself.

**Getting an education is your responsibility, whether you studied in school or not.**

### ➵ The “Five-Year Rule” of Growth

Think back to yourself five years ago. You probably laugh at the things you once believed. That’s not stupidity — it’s **evolution**. As the “Five-Year Rule” suggests, the very things we once held as absolute truths are often quickly superseded by new insights and experiences. Acknowledging this fluid nature of knowledge is the first step to keeping our minds open and hungry for growth. And the wild part? This doesn’t just happen every five years. It’s happening **right now, moment by moment.** The more aware you are, the faster you evolve.

### ➵ What To Do?

1. 🚀 <strong>Practice Active Listening</strong><br>Most people listen to respond, not to understand. Next time you’re in a conversation, resist the urge to mentally craft your rebuttal. Instead, focus entirely on what the other person is saying. Even if you disagree, listen deeply. You might learn something unexpected.
2. 🔎 <strong>Seek Out Disconfirming Evidence</strong><br>Your brain loves confirmation bias — it seeks information that supports what you already believe. Break this cycle. **Deliberately consume content that challenges your views.** Read opposing perspectives, debate with people who think differently, and expose yourself to ideas that make you uncomfortable. Growth happens in discomfort.
3. 📖 **Keep Growth Mindset** <br>Reflect on moments when your thinking has shifted. Reminisce times you’ve been wrong, insights that changed your perspective, and areas where you’ve evolved. Looking back, you’ll see proof that growth isn’t about knowing — it’s about learning.
4. ❓ <strong>Ask, “What if I’m wrong?”</strong><br>Whenever you feel confident about something, pause and ask: **“What if my understanding is incomplete?”** This question alone forces you into a more curious and open-minded state. Certainty closes doors; questioning opens them.

### ➵ Say “I Don’t Know” more often

If remembering everything above feels like too much work, because, let’s be honest, your brain already struggles to remember why you started reading this. Was it the short read time? The cool artwork? Or just because you know me? None of those are good enough. Read this for yourself — because you **want** to grow, not because you got baited into it.

Just remember this: say **“I don’t know”** more often. It’s not an admission of ignorance — it’s a signal to your brain to **shut up and listen.** It tells your ego to take a seat, makes you okay with being wrong, and most importantly, keeps you open to growth.

Because here’s the thing — if you truly know something, why the desperate need for validation? **Why waste energy proving yourself when real understanding is its own reward?**

So, next time you feel the need to assert your knowledge, try this instead: **Pause. Listen. Reflect.** And when in doubt, just say it — **“I don’t know.”** That simple phrase might just be the smartest thing you ever say. 😉

Stay curious. Stay evolving. 🚀
